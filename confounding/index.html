Four ways of handling a third variable, and what each of them is actually doing to the number you report.

What you do What it gives you Measured here Watch out for
Compare the groups The association, which answers a different question Measured above. It is the right answer only when nothing decides treatment.
Adjust for the confounder The effect, if you adjusted for the right thing and measured it well Measured above. Coarse groups and noisy measurements both leave part of the bias behind.
Adjust for everything Whatever the graph says it gives you Measured above. A common effect of treatment and outcome creates bias where there was none.
Draw the graph first A decision the data cannot make for you Measured above. Two graphs can fit the same data and disagree about the answer.
