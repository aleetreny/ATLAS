Two ways to start from a model that already exists: inherit what it learned to see, or inherit what it answers. Both work, and both are usually reported without the control that says why

Module 8.1 · Tuning · Transfer Learning and Distillation

The AutoML article ended on a cheap trick: a system that has seen other people's tables arrives at yours already knowing four configurations worth trying. This one is the same idea with the weights instead of the configuration, and it comes in two flavours that get discussed as if they were one.


Transfer learning reuses a model's representations: train on one task, freeze the middle, and put a new head on it. Distillation reuses a model's answers: train a small model to reproduce a big one's output distribution rather than the labels. Both are well established, so the interesting question is not whether they work. It is what they are being compared against, because in both cases the usual comparison is missing the control that decides what the mechanism is.

How Much to Thaw

Learning From an Answer Instead of a Label


The student is trained on the teacher's probabilities rather than the label, with both sides read at a temperature: $$p_i(T) = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}$$ A high temperature flattens the distribution, which pulls the small differences between the wrong classes up into a range where a gradient can see them. Those gradients shrink like \(1/T^2\), which is why the loss is multiplied back by \(T^2\) so that changing the temperature changes what is being taught and not how fast.
