What A Word Is Worth

Turning a document into a vector is three separate decisions, and the one that is not in the name does most of the work

aleetreny

Module 4.1 · NLP · What A Word Is Worth

Everything in the first two modules of this atlas started from a row of numbers. A flower had four measurements, a wine had thirteen, a house had a price. A document has none. It is a sequence of words, of no fixed length, drawn from a set nobody wrote down, and before any model can touch it somebody has to decide what its numbers are.


The oldest answer is still the one to beat: count the words.


That answer has a name, TF-IDF, and the name has two parts, and almost every description of it treats those two parts as the whole thing. They are not. There is a third decision hiding in the formula that nobody put in the name, and this article measures all three separately, on the same corpus and the same split, because measuring them together is how a folklore survives for fifty years.

Three Decisions, One Name

Written out in full, the weight of one term in one document is this:

$$w_{t,d} = \underbrace{f(c_{t,d})}_{\text{what to do with the count}} \times \underbrace{\left(\ln \frac{1+N}{1+\mathrm{df}_t} + 1\right)}_{\text{weight by rarity}} \Bigg/ \underbrace{\left\lVert \cdot \right\rVert_2}_{\text{divide by the length}}$$

Three knobs, and only two of them are in the name.


The widget below recomputes all of it here, from the integer counts, for one document at a time. Changing the first knob reorders the bars. Changing the second reorders them again, usually the other way. The third cannot reorder anything, because it divides every bar by the same number, which is exactly why it is easy to believe it does not matter.

The Verdict

Three decisions, measured one at a time, on one corpus and one task.

Decision What it is for What it bought here Watch out for
What to do with the count Stopping one word repeated forty times from drowning out forty different words Measured above. It interacts with the other two.
Weight by rarity Damping words that are everywhere and so tell you nothing Measured above. It has never seen your labels.
Divide by the length Comparing documents on what they are about rather than how long they are Measured above. It is not in the name.
Pairs of adjacent words Putting back some of the order a bag of words throws away Measured above. Most of them are never seen twice.

The counting model has a ceiling, and it is not where you would guess.
