Barely Moving the Weights

Adapting a model to a new task changes its weight matrices, and the change turns out to have a shape. Measuring that shape is what makes it possible to train a thousandth of the parameters and land in the same place

aleetreny

Module 8.1 · Tuning · LoRA and QLoRA

The transfer and distillation article ended with a dial that nobody has a principled way to set: how many layers of an inherited model to let the gradient into. LoRA answers that question by refusing it. Do not choose layers. Let the gradient reach every matrix, but only through a narrow slot.


The construction is one line. Freeze the pretrained matrix \(W_0\), and write the adapted one as $$W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r}\,BA, \qquad A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},\; B \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k},\; r \ll d$$ with \(B\) starting at zero so the model begins as an exact copy of what it was. Only \(A\) and \(B\) are trained. Everything interesting about this rests on one empirical claim, which is that the thing being approximated, the update \(W - W_0\), really is close to rank \(r\) for small \(r\). That is a claim about a matrix, so it can be measured.
