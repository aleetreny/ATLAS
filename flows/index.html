Module 3.2 · Probabilistic Generative Models · Change of Variables

Take a standard gaussian and push it through an invertible function. The density of what comes out is not an approximation and not a bound: it is the density you started with, divided by how much the function stretched space at that point. That is the change of variables formula, it is an identity, and it has been in every probability textbook for a century.


Using it as a model means building a network that is invertible and whose stretching factor can be computed. Both are real restrictions and this article measures what each one costs, starting with the one that never appears in the papers: a smooth invertible map cannot tear a hole in anything, and the density this module is fitted against has one.
