Module 3.3 · Diffusion · Few Step Sampling

Every sampler in this module has had the same shape. Start from noise, ask a network what to do, take a small step, repeat. The pixel article ran two hundred of those steps and the conditioning article made each one cost two network passes instead of one. That is four hundred passes for a picture the decoder then renders in a single pass, and it is the reason diffusion models are slow in a way that has nothing to do with how big they are.


The obvious response is to take fewer, larger steps. It does not work, and the reason it does not work is not a subtlety of the algorithm: it is a fact about the shape of the path the sampler is walking. This article measures that shape, and then measures what three different attempts to change it are actually worth.

Retrain It On Its Own Output


Why that should straighten anything is worth a sentence. The training target is the velocity of the straight line joining a pair, and when the pairs are independent, many different lines run through the same point in different directions. The network cannot satisfy all of them, so it learns their average, and the average of a fan of directions is not the direction of any of them: the field bends. Pairing noise with the sample it actually produced removes the crossings, and a field with nothing to average is free to be straight.
