Module 3.2 · Probabilistic Generative Models · Unnormalised Densities

The two models so far were built the same way: an encoder, a decoder, and a distribution over the thing in between. Both of them arranged their architecture so that sampling would be easy, and paid for it, the first with a bound whose looseness nobody knows and the second with a gradient that does not exist.


There is a way to stop paying either price. Write a function, any function, from a point to a number. Call low values likely and high values unlikely. Divide by the integral so it sums to one. That is a density, it can be as expressive as the network you wrote, and it needs no encoder, no invertibility and no architectural concession at all.
