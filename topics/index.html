Module 4.1 · NLP · What It Is About

The previous article left a document as a point in a space with one axis per word.


A topic model proposes a different shape for the answer. Rather than a document being a list of words, it is a mixture of a small number of topics, and a topic is a distribution over words. Nobody says what the topics are. They are inferred from nothing but which words turn up together, and that is simultaneously the appeal and the problem.


Almost every article about topic models evaluates them by printing the topics and inviting you to nod.


The sampler runs in this page.

A Story About How A Document Got Written

The model is a claim about a process. To write a document, first draw a mixture over topics, then for each word in turn draw a topic from that mixture and a word from that topic:

$$\theta_d \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha), \qquad z_{d,i} \sim \theta_d, \qquad w_{d,i} \sim \phi_{z_{d,i}}, \qquad \phi_t \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\beta)$$

Nobody believes this is how anything was written.


Inference is the interesting part.

The blocks appear. Whether they are the right blocks is a separate question.

The Verdict

One model, one corpus with a key, and the four knobs that decided more than the model did.

Decision What it looks like What it did here Watch out for
The filtering Two lines before the model runs Measured above. It is not housekeeping.
The number of topics An integer you supply Measured above. Two criteria, two answers.
The seed Not a modelling decision at all Measured above. Nobody reports it.
The document prior A default in the library Measured above. It decides how mixed a document is allowed to be.
