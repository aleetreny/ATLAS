Somewhere to Sample From

The bottleneck article left a decoder that answers anywhere in the plane, and no reason to believe that a point picked at random is somewhere it answers well; this one puts a distribution there on purpose and measures what that costs and what it buys

aleetreny

Module 3.2 · Probabilistic Generative Models · Latent Variables

The autoencoders article ended on a promise it could not keep. Its decoder is a function from two numbers to a picture, it answers at points where no digit ever was, and that is most of a way to generate digits: pick a point, decode it, look at what comes out. What was missing was any reason to believe that a picked point lands somewhere the decoder handles well.


That is a question about the distribution of the code, and nothing in a reconstruction loss ever mentions one. So the first thing to do is stop arguing and try it. The network below is not a new one: it is that article's own trained network, read out of its own published file, weight for weight, and asked to decode points drawn from a Gaussian fitted to the codes it produces.

The Two Terms Pull Against Each Other

Written down, the model is a prior over codes and a decoder that turns a code into a distribution over pictures, and what we want is the probability it assigns to a real one:

$$ p_\theta(x) = \int p(z)\, p_\theta(x \mid z)\, dz, \qquad p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, I) $$

$$ \log p_\theta(x) \;\ge\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z \mid x)}\!\left[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)\right]}_{\text{rebuild it}} \;-\; \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}\!\left(q_\phi(z \mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\right)}_{\text{and stay where sampling happens}} $$
