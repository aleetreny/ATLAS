Two articles of this branch have now represented a document by counting its words, and both have run into the same wall.


The repair is a sentence from a linguist, written in 1957 and quoted ever since: you shall know a word by the company it keeps.


Three models, one corpus, and all three run in this page.

What Is Actually Being Optimised

Skip-gram with negative sampling is usually described as a neural network, which makes it sound more mysterious than it is.

$$\ell = \log \sigma(v_w \cdot v_c) + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \mathbb{E}_{c_i \sim P_n} \log \sigma(-v_w \cdot v_{c_i})$$

And there is a theorem about where that lands.

Which reframes the whole thing.

The Verdict

Three ways to give a word a position, measured on one tagged corpus.

Reach for When What it bought here Watch out for
Skip-gram You have a corpus and want vectors out of it without thinking hard Measured above. It has no answer for a word it never saw.
GloVe The co-occurrence matrix fits in memory and you would rather fit it once than stream pairs Measured above. Same hard edge on unseen words.
FastText Morphology matters, or the vocabulary is open, which it always is Measured above. Character n-grams tie words that only look alike.
The window You care whether "similar" means same-role or same-topic Measured above. It is reported as a detail and behaves as a definition.

A word now has a position, and the position knows things.


What none of it knows is where the word was.

(Mikolov, T., Sutskever, I., Chen, K., Corrado, G. & Dean, J., NIPS, 2013; skip-gram with negative sampling, the subsampling of frequent words and the three-quarter power noise distribution, all three implemented here)

(Bojanowski, P., Grave, E., Joulin, A. & Mikolov, T., TACL, 2017; the character n-grams that give an unseen word a vector)

[5] Improving Distributional Similarity with Lessons Learned from Word Embeddings
(Levy, O., Goldberg, Y. & Dagan, I., TACL, 2015; that most of the reported gap between these methods is hyperparameters, which is why everything here shares them)
