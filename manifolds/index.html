Module 2.2 · Dimensionality Reduction · Manifold Learning

Every method in this module so far has made the same assumption without ever announcing it. PCA, ICA and NMF write a row as a weighted sum of a few atoms, which says the data lies near a flat slice of the space it was measured in. Classical scaling looks for coordinates whose straight line distances reproduce a table. Metric scaling minimises a residual against straight line distances in the map. Different machinery, one shared belief: that the distance between two rows is the distance through the space they arrived in.


Suppose it is not. Suppose the data lies on a curved sheet, the way the pixels of a face rotating through a hundred angles lie on a curve with one degree of freedom inside a space with ten thousand. Then two rows can be close together in the room and very far apart along the sheet, and no amount of rotating the room will fix it, because the thing you want is not a direction at all. It is a path.
