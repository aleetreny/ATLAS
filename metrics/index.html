Module 8.2 · Evaluation · ROC-AUC, MCC and BLEU

The cross validation article was about the split. This one is about what gets computed once the split has done its job, and it has a similar shape: the default is chosen by convention, it answers a specific question, and the question is rarely the one being asked.


Three metrics, picked because they fail in three different ways. The area under the ROC curve is a statement about an ordering and about nothing else. The Matthews correlation coefficient is a statement about a two by two table, in competition with several others that summarise the same table differently. BLEU is a statement about overlapping word sequences, standing in for a judgement about meaning.


An Area That Is Really a Count


The area under the curve of true positives against false positives has a second definition that does not mention a curve at all: it is the probability that a randomly chosen positive is scored above a randomly chosen negative, with ties counted as half. Written as a count over pairs, $$\mathrm{AUC} = \frac{1}{n_{+}n_{-}}\sum_{i \in +}\sum_{j \in -} \left[\,\mathbb{1}(s_i > s_j) + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{1}(s_i = s_j)\right]$$ which is the Mann and Whitney statistic.
