Module 3.2 · Probabilistic Generative Models · Flows on Images

The previous article ended with an exact number of nats on a two dimensional density. Nats on a plane are hard to feel. Nats on an image are not: divide by the number of pixels, convert to base two, and an exact log likelihood becomes bits per pixel, which is a compression rate, which is a thing gzip also produces.


Glow is the architecture that made flows work on pictures, and it is three pieces stacked: a per channel scale and shift, a learned mixing of the channels, and a coupling layer. Each is ablated below on the same data with the same budget, and each has a floor under its verdict.
