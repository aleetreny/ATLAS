Where the Vectors Live

An index assumes the collection sits still, the query is only a vector, and the nearest vectors are what you wanted. A database gets none of those, and each one that goes breaks something measurable

Module 8.3 · Vector Search · Vector Databases

The previous article built three indexes over a pile of vectors that never changed, answered questions of the form "the ten nearest", and scored them against exhaustive search. Everything it measured holds. What it left out is everything that makes the difference between an index and a system somebody runs.


Three assumptions go, one at a time. The collection changes, so vectors arrive and leave. The query carries a condition, so the answer must come from a subset chosen by something that is not a vector. And the thing being retrieved is a document rather than a vector, so a word that matches exactly can beat a direction that is merely close.

The fusion rule is deliberately the crudest one that works. Reciprocal rank fusion throws away every score and keeps only the position each system gave a document: $$\mathrm{RRF}(d) = \sum_{s} \frac{1}{k + \mathrm{rank}_s(d)}$$ which needs no calibration between a similarity and a term weight, and is the reason it survives contact with two systems whose numbers mean nothing to each other.
