A row of a table is usually exchangeable with any other row: shuffle the training set and nothing is lost. That is the assumption underneath cross validation, underneath bootstrapping, and underneath most of what the previous four modules did. A series breaks it. Row 300 comes after row 299, the only rows you may learn from are the ones above the line, and the thing you are asked to predict is the one thing you have never seen: the future.

Reading a Correlogram

The classical way to choose a model is to plot two functions of the data and read the order off them. The autocorrelation at lag $k$ is the correlation of the series with itself shifted by $k$; the partial autocorrelation is what is left of it once the shorter lags have had their turn:

$$ \rho_k = \frac{\sum_{t=k+1}^{n}(y_t-\bar y)(y_{t-k}-\bar y)}{\sum_{t=1}^{n}(y_t-\bar y)^2}, \qquad \phi_{kk} = \operatorname{corr}\left(y_t,\, y_{t-k} \mid y_{t-1},\dots,y_{t-k+1}\right) $$

What you have Reach for Why Watch out for

A number to put in a report A rolling origin, never a random split
