After Depth

The same compute, spent differently: where to put a budget, what a modern block is made of, and how much of an ablation table is noise

aleetreny

Module 1.3 · Computer Vision · Architecture

The previous article ended where the depth race did. Once a shortcut made a network of any depth trainable, adding layers stopped being an idea and became a dial, and turning it further stopped paying. What followed were two different answers to the same question: given a fixed amount of computation, what is the best thing to do with it?


EfficientNet's answer, in 2019, was about allocation: a network has three dimensions you can grow, depth, width and input resolution, and there is a right ratio between them. ConvNeXt's answer, in 2022, was about arrangement: take a residual network and apply, one at a time, every design change the transformer era had made ordinary, and see how far a convolutional network gets.


Both are ablation tables, and ablation tables are where this atlas has to be careful. A row that says "+0.4 points" means nothing without knowing what the same experiment gives when you change only the random seed. So before anything else, this page measures that, at three different model sizes, and every result below is drawn against it.

The Axis That Costs No Parameters

Of the three dimensions, resolution is the strange one. Growing depth or width adds weights; growing the input does not add a single one, and still costs more to run. That makes it the cheapest axis to scale and the easiest one to misread, so this ladder ships with two controls.

The Saving That Is Capped by the Kernel

One rung of that ladder deserves its own section, because it is the change that made all the others affordable. A dense convolution mixes space and channels in one operation and costs \(k^2 C_{\text{in}} C_{\text{out}}\). A depthwise separable convolution splits it in two, one filter per channel and then a 1 × 1 mixing step, for \(k^2 C_{\text{in}} + C_{\text{in}} C_{\text{out}}\).


Drive both sliders. The ratio falls as the channels grow and then stops falling, and where it stops is set by the kernel alone.

The Verdict

What survives from both papers at this scale, and what does not.

Idea What it buys Measured here Watch out for
Compound scaling A rule for splitting a budget between depth, width and resolution Seven allocations of the same budget, with the spread between best and worst reported in the chart above. The exponents multiply the cost by 1.92 per step, not 2, and integer block counts mean the arms never land on the same budget anyway.
Resolution Capacity without parameters A ladder from the smallest input to the largest, with a compute control and an information control beside it. A resolution ladder alone cannot separate more information from more computation. That is what the two controls are for.
Depthwise separable Most of the arithmetic, gone The closed form, driven live, with its 1/k² floor; and a stopwatch that disagrees with it. Fewer multiply-accumulates is not fewer seconds. On this machine the disagreement moves with the thread count, so the article states that they disagree and refuses to state by how much.
The modernisation ladder A path from a 2015 block to a 2022 one Eleven rungs against a measured noise floor, with the count of rungs that cleared it stated above. The rungs are cumulative and they interact: the parts do not add up to the whole, and the gap is measured rather than assumed away.
