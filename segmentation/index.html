Which Pixels

A box says an object is somewhere inside a rectangle. Segmentation says which pixels, and on these garments that turns out to be a different question with a different answer

aleetreny

Module 1.3 · Computer Vision · Segmentation

The previous article ended with a detector drawing boxes, and a box is a strange thing to be satisfied with. It says the object is somewhere in this rectangle. It does not say where in the rectangle, or what shape it is, or where it stops.


This article runs on exactly the same pictures, and the first thing it does is put a number on that complaint. Not on a detector's boxes: on the perfect boxes, the ground truth the last article scored against.

The Verdict

What each idea in this article is worth, at this scale, on these pictures.

Idea What it buys Measured here Watch out for
A mask instead of a box The shape of the thing, not the rectangle around it Measured above. Masks cost annotation that boxes do not, and everything on this page is easier than reality because the masks here were generated rather than drawn by a person.
Skip connections The resolution the encoder threw away, handed back to the decoder Measured above. Ablating them by deleting them also changes the parameter count and the arithmetic. Zero them instead, or the comparison is not about skips.
Instances, not just classes An answer to "how many", which a per-pixel map has no room for Measured above. The mask head is only as good as the features it pools from. Here it pools from an eighth-resolution map, so its ceiling is set before it is trained.
Promptable segmentation A model that answers about things rather than about categories Measured above. Five held-out garment classes are a much softer test than the open world. This page can show the property exists; it cannot show it survives at SAM's scale.

Six articles ago a photograph was turned into histograms of gradients by hand; this one ends with a network that takes a point and returns a shape, and every step in between was measured on the same kind of picture rather than quoted.
