Module 8.1 · Tuning · Grid, Random and Bayesian Search

Seventy eight articles have now fitted something, and almost every one of them had a number nobody chose on purpose: a depth, a penalty, a width, a learning rate. The usual sentence is "chosen by cross validation on a grid", and it hides two different questions. How do you decide where to look, and how much of what you find is real?


Both questions are answerable here for a reason most writing on the subject cannot afford. The search space on this page is small enough to enumerate completely, so there is no argument about who won: every cell has been measured, twice by cross validation and once on data no search is allowed to see.

Fitting a Model of the Surface


The recipe is the one every Bayesian optimiser uses. Fit a Gaussian process to the points already evaluated, which gives a predicted score and an uncertainty everywhere else, and then evaluate wherever expected improvement is largest: $$\mathrm{EI}(x) = (\mu(x) - y^{*})\,\Phi(z) + \sigma(x)\,\phi(z), \qquad z = \frac{\mu(x) - y^{*}}{\sigma(x)}$$ The first term wants a high predicted score and the second wants an uncertain one, which is the whole exploration and exploitation trade in one line. Since the space here is a finite grid, there is nothing to optimise about the acquisition itself: it is evaluated in all 961 cells and the largest wins.
