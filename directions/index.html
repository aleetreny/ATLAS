Directions

One factorisation, three constraints: ask it for orthogonality, for independence, or for nothing negative, and it hands back three different worlds

aleetreny

Module 2.2 · Dimensionality Reduction · Linear Factorisations

The previous article closed the clustering branch and then pointed sideways. Every method in that branch assumed the structure worth finding is groups: this bottle belongs with those bottles. Sometimes it is not groups. Sometimes the structure is directions, the handful of combinations along which the data actually varies, and the thirteen numbers you measured are thirteen noisy views of three or four things.


Three methods open this module, and it is worth seeing early that they are not three ideas. They are one idea and three constraints. Each one takes the data matrix and splits it into a small dictionary of building blocks and the amount of each block every row uses:

$$ \underbrace{X}_{n \times p} \;\approx\; \underbrace{W}_{n \times k}\;\underbrace{H}_{k \times p} \qquad k \ll p $$

The rows of \(H\) are the building blocks, the atoms. The rows of \(W\) are the recipes: how much of each atom this particular row is made of. Pick \(k\) far smaller than \(p\) and you have compressed the data; pick the atoms well and you have explained it. Written like this, principal component analysis, independent component analysis and non-negative matrix factorisation are the same sentence with three different endings.


What separates them is what you are allowed to demand of \(W\) and \(H\), and the whole article is about how much that choice changes. PCA wants the atoms perpendicular to each other and ordered by how much variance they capture. ICA does not care about variance at all and wants the recipes statistically independent. NMF forbids a single negative number anywhere, so nothing can ever be built by cancellation. Three constraints, three answers, and one dataset at a time to tell them apart.


The Best Line

Start with the smallest version of the problem that still has one: two numbers per bottle, and the question of whether one would do. These are the same 178 wines the clustering branch kept coming back to, and for now only two of their thirteen measurements.

Two Numbers Per Bottle

Flavanoid content across the bottom, colour absorbance up the side, one dot per wine. The cloud leans, which is another way of saying the two measurements are not telling you two independent things. Both axes are drawn at the same scale, so an angle on the screen is an angle in the data.

Pick a Direction, Any Direction

A line through the middle of the cloud, and every bottle's shadow on it: the closest point of the line to that bottle. Reading a wine off that line means one number instead of two. The question is which line, and what "best" could possibly mean before anyone has said what the number is for.

What the Shadow Costs

The pink stubs are what the shadow throws away: the part of each bottle at right angles to the line, which the single number cannot carry. Add up their squared lengths and you have the reconstruction error, the honest price of the compression.

One Number Buys the Other

Now turn the line and watch two quantities move: the spread of the shadows, and the total length of the stubs. They are not two quantities. Every unit of variance the line captures is a unit of error it does not pay, exactly, at every angle, because the shadow and the stub meet at a right angle and Pythagoras does the rest.

The Best Line Has a Name

That equivalence is worth stating once in symbols, because it is the reason a single method answers two different questions. For a unit direction \(u\) and centred data:

$$ \underbrace{\operatorname{Var}(Xu)}_{\text{captured}} \;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i \lVert x_i - (x_i^{\top} u)\,u \rVert^2}_{\text{thrown away}} \;=\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i \lVert x_i \rVert^2}_{\text{total, and fixed}} $$

What a Covariance Cannot Know

PCA looks at exactly one thing: the covariance matrix. Means and pairwise co-variation, and nothing else. That is an enormous amount of information and it is also a hard ceiling, because plenty of very different datasets share a covariance matrix, and PCA cannot tell them apart even in principle. Here is the classic case where the difference is the entire point.



Whitening is where PCA's contribution ends and where the interesting part starts. Once the data has been rotated onto its principal axes and each axis divided by its own standard deviation, the covariance is the identity matrix, and it stays the identity matrix under any further rotation whatsoever. Second-order statistics have said everything they have to say, and there is exactly one degree of freedom left. So put it on a dial.

The Verdict

Same equation, three constraints, and the constraint is the model.

Decision Default Why Watch out for
Standardise first? Yes, unless the units are already comparable Standardising is itself a modelling choice: it declares every measurement equally important, which is a claim, not a neutral act.
Choosing k Cumulative variance, then check the task
Compression PCA, and nothing else The atoms are ordered by variance and blind to your labels, and the sign of each one is arbitrary.
Separating mixed sources ICA, after PCA has whitened
Atoms a person must read NMF, when the data is non-negative
