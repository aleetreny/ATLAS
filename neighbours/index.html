Module 2.2 · Dimensionality Reduction · Neighbour Embeddings

Everything in this module so far has been an argument about distances. PCA minimises squared error against them. Classical scaling inverts them. Metric scaling chases them. Isomap replaces them with better ones and then inverts those. All four hand back a map whose distances are supposed to mean something, and all four are judged on how well they manage it.


There is a reason that project is doomed before it starts, and it has nothing to do with the algorithms. It is a fact about planes.


What a Plane Cannot Hold

Ask a Different Question

t-SNE's answer is to stop asking for distances at all. It converts the data into a probability distribution over pairs, converts a candidate map into a second one, and moves the map until the two distributions agree. There are five steps and each of them is a decision that shows up later.

Which Is All Of It

In symbols, and there are only three lines. The neighbour distribution in the data, the one in the map, and the thing being minimised:

$$ p_{j|i} = \frac{\exp(-\lVert x_i - x_j \rVert^2 / 2\sigma_i^2)}{\sum_{k \neq i}\exp(-\lVert x_i - x_k \rVert^2 / 2\sigma_i^2)}, \qquad p_{ij} = \frac{p_{j|i} + p_{i|j}}{2n} $$ $$ q_{ij} = \frac{(1 + \lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^2)^{-1}}{\sum_{k \neq l}(1 + \lVert y_k - y_l \rVert^2)^{-1}}, \qquad C = \sum_{i \neq j} p_{ij}\log\frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}} $$
